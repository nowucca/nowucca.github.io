Stop Chasing It. Build the Road.

The life you want isn't a finish line you sprint toward. It's what happens by default once your days are built for it.

Tuesday 17 November 20262 min read aloudMake it inevitable

You've probably spent a lot of energy wanting a different life. The body, the business, the calm mornings, the work you're proud of. And wanting it hard hasn't got you there, because wanting is a feeling, and feelings come and go.

So try something different this week. Stop chasing the life and start building the road that leads to it.

Picture water on a hillside. It doesn't try to reach the valley. It just follows the shape of the ground. Your days work the same way. You drift toward whatever is easiest, closest and most familiar. If the phone is on the pillow, you scroll. If the running shoes are by the door, you run. That isn't weakness. It's gravity.

Which means the real work isn't summoning more willpower. It's reshaping the ground.

Start with who you are, not what you want. "I want to write a book" is a wish. "I'm a writer, and writers write before breakfast" is a decision about identity, and every page after that is a vote for it. You don't need a hundred votes today. You need one.

Then make the right move the easy one. Put the guitar on its stand, not in the case. Book the gym class so cancelling feels like more work than going. Delete the app that eats your evenings. Every bit of friction you remove from the good choice, and every bit you add to the bad one, does the job willpower was never built for.

And make it small enough that you can't fail. Ten minutes. One page. One call. Small actions repeated every day beat heroic ones that happen twice.

Here's the quiet truth: the people living the life you want aren't more motivated than you. Their days are just arranged so that the next right step is the obvious one.

You don't have to chase the dream. Build the road, take one step a day, and let the slope do the rest.

This week

Pick one habit your future self would have. Tonight, change one thing in your space so tomorrow's version of it takes less than two minutes to start.
