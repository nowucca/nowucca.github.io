Get In the Water

Readiness isn't the ticket you need before you begin. It's the feeling you get a few minutes after you do.

Monday 23 November 20262 min read aloudStart before you're ready

Think of the edge of a cold lake on an early summer morning. You stand there with your toes on the wet rock. You tell yourself you'll go in once you're ready. And you keep standing there.

Here's what everyone who swims there knows. The water never gets warmer while you wait. You don't feel ready, and then jump. You jump, you gasp, and about a minute later you feel fine. The readiness arrives after you're in. Not before.

Most of the things you've been putting off work the same way.

You're waiting to feel ready to start the project, ask for the meeting, post the first piece, have the honest conversation. You think readiness is something you collect first. More research. One more course. A clearer week. But readiness isn't a box you tick at the start. It's a feeling you earn by doing the thing badly a few times.

And waiting has a cost you don't see. Every week on the rock, the water looks a little colder. The story in your head gets louder. The gap between you and the thing grows, even though nothing about the thing has changed.

So lower the bar for what counts as starting. You don't need the finished plan. You need a rough first version that you can look at. One ugly paragraph. One sketch on the back of an envelope. One message that says, "Can we talk on Thursday?" Starting isn't a promise to be good at it. It's a promise to find out.

You'll feel the shock. That's normal. It means you've moved from imagining to doing. Stay in for a minute. Notice that you're still breathing. Notice that the thing you dreaded is smaller up close.

The people you admire weren't ready either. They just got used to the cold first.

You will never feel fully prepared. That isn't a sign to wait. It's just what the edge feels like. Step off it.

This week

Name the one thing you've been waiting to feel ready for. Before Wednesday night, make a deliberately rough first attempt at it in 20 minutes or less, then show it to one person by Friday.
