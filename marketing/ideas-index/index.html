1. The hook rule: curiosity, tension, emotion

Film keeps an audience in its seat with three forces working together. Use them in the first line of anything: a post, a reel, a subject line, a landing page headline.

Curiosity

Open a question and don't answer it yet. Hint at a secret, a result or a twist the reader can only get by staying.

Tension

Put something at stake: a conflict, a deadline, a risk, or the gap between where someone is and where they want to be.

Emotion

Make it matter to a person. Tie the stakes to a feeling the audience already knows: fear, relief, pride, embarrassment, hope.

Checklist for every hook. Does it leave a question open? Is something at risk? Will the reader feel something? In a long intro use all three, in that order. In a one-liner, pair curiosity with at least one of the others. Then pay it off: a hook that never answers its question burns trust.

3. Content ideas that never run dry

Evergreen angles that work on any platform. Each one has a sample hook to show how the three levers apply.

Origin story

Why you started, what you were running from, what you were chasing.

"I almost didn't build this. The night I decided to was the worst week of my career." CE

Mistake and lesson

A real failure, what it cost, and the rule you live by now.

"This mistake cost us $40k and three months. It was completely avoidable." CTE

Behind the scenes

The messy middle: drafts, tools, the process nobody normally sees.

"Here's what launch day actually looked like, including the part where everything broke." CT

Myth-busting

A belief your audience holds that is costing them, and the evidence against it.

"'Just be consistent' is the most expensive marketing advice I ever followed." CT

Contrarian take

Disagree with your field's received wisdom and back it up.

"Everyone says niche down. I did the opposite and it worked. Here's when that's true." CT

Customer or case story

Someone's problem, the turning point, the result. The customer is the hero; you are the guide.

"She was a week from shutting the shop down. Then one change to her homepage..." CTE

Teardown

Pick apart a famous ad, landing page, product launch or post and show why it works.

"This 9-word headline made a company millions. Two of the words are doing all the work." C

How-to tutorial

A specific outcome in a few steps. Lead with the pain the outcome removes.

"If your inbox owns your mornings, this 10-minute setup gets them back." TE

Data reveal

Original numbers from your own work, a survey, or an experiment.

"We looked at 1,000 cold emails. The ones that got replies all did one strange thing." C

Prediction

Where your field goes next and what people should do now.

"In two years half of today's SEO playbook won't work. Here's what replaces it." CT

Day in the life

Show the routine, the tools and the moments that surprise people.

"The part of my job nobody warned me about happens every day at 4 p.m." CE

FAQ answered honestly

The question every prospect asks, answered with more candor than competitors dare.

"People ask why we're more expensive. The honest answer isn't 'quality'." CT

Before and after

Show the transformation side by side, then explain the change.

"Same product, same price. One version sold three times as many. Spot the difference?" C

Hot seat or Q&A

Answer audience questions live or on camera; the audience supplies the tension.

"You asked me the question I've been avoiding for a year. Okay. Here goes." CTE

Series and cliffhangers

Break a story into parts and end each one on an open question, exactly like an episode.

"Part 2: the investor said yes. Then he asked for something I couldn't give." CT

5. Repurposing ladder

Make one substantial piece, then cut it down. Each rung gets its own hook rather than a copy of the original's.

  1. Pillar: a long video, podcast episode, webinar or in-depth article.
  2. Mid-form: a blog post, newsletter issue and LinkedIn carousel from the pillar's main points.
  3. Short-form: 3–5 vertical clips, each built around one moment of tension.
  4. Micro: quote cards, single-line posts, polls and a thread.
  5. Recycle: revisit winners every 3–6 months with a new hook and updated examples.

6. Distribution and growth

  • Collaborations: guest posts, podcast swaps, co-hosted lives, Instagram Collab posts, newsletter cross-recommendations.
  • Lead magnets: a checklist, template, mini-course or tool in exchange for an email address. The title is a hook too.
  • Launch sequences: tease (curiosity), reveal the problem (tension), show the people it helps (emotion), open the doors, close with a real deadline.
  • Referral loops: reward customers and subscribers for sharing; make the shareable thing worth sharing on its own.
  • Search: keyword research on the questions people already ask; one page per question, answered thoroughly.
  • Comment strategy: thoughtful replies on larger accounts in your niche; often more reach than your own posts early on.
  • Paid amplification: boost only what already performs organically; test hooks as ad variants.
  • Events: workshops, webinars, meetups and talks; record everything and feed the repurposing ladder.
  • Owned audience first: every platform is rented, so move followers onto an email list.

7. Turning attention into action

  • Landing page arc: headline (hook) → the problem in the reader's words → the turning point (your offer) → proof → what happens next → call to action.
  • One call to action per piece. Tell people exactly what to do and what they get.
  • Social proof: testimonials told as mini-stories (before, turning point, after) beat star ratings.
  • Offers: make the first step small: a free trial, an audit, a sample, a short call.
  • Honest urgency: real deadlines and limits only. Fake scarcity spends trust.
  • Objection handling: answer the top three reasons people don't buy, right on the page.

8. Measuring what works

StageSignalWhat it tells you
Hook3-second view rate, scroll-stop rate, open rate, click-through on "see more"Whether curiosity and tension landed
StoryAverage watch time, completion rate, read depthWhether the middle kept its promise
ResonanceSaves, shares, replies, DMsWhether it hit an emotion strongly enough to pass on
ActionClicks, sign-ups, conversion rate, revenue per visitorWhether attention turned into a result

Test one variable at a time. Keep the body the same and try three hooks: one led by curiosity, one by tension, one by emotion. Keep a swipe file of winners and why they won.

9. A weekly rhythm

  • Monday: plan the week's pillar and pick its three strongest moments.
  • Tuesday–Wednesday: make the pillar.
  • Thursday: cut it down the repurposing ladder; write a fresh hook for each piece.
  • Friday: publish the newsletter; review last week's numbers; add winners to the swipe file.
  • Daily, 20 minutes: reply to comments and engage in two or three communities.
