  1. 2026-11-24R285Pachocki: OpenAI proposes perplexity as the shared yardstick for frontier pretrainingOpenAI's chief scientist argues that most of what models can do comes from pretraining and that post-training mostly draws out what is already there, so held-out perplexity is the most direct single measure of underlying capability and labs should report it on a common basis. He publishes OpenAI's internal ladder in log-perplexity: about 0.99 for fluent chat (GPT-3.5), about 0.91 where chain-of-thought becomes productive (GPT-4), below 0.8 for emergent cybersecurity and mathematical capability, about 0.76 where a model began producing results on open math problems, and “transformative capabilities” expected around 0.68 to 0.7, possibly as soon as 2027. The policy ask is shared thresholds at which labs agree to extra safeguards. Key caveats: it is a position essay from a lab proposing a metric it already reports well on; perplexity is hard to compare across tokenisers and held-out sets, is detached from task evals, and does not fall when a model reasons at inference; and the 0.76 claim and the 2027 timeline are assertions without published evidence.
