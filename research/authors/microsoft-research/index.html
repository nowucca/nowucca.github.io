  1. 2026-11-23R239sharedDynamic Expert Pruning: per-agent MoE expert masks from the agent's own promptsMoE models save compute but not memory, and static expert pruning applies one offline mask to every request, which fails when one backbone serves many agent roles. Dynamic Expert Pruning finds that an agent's system and task prompts predict which experts it needs: a lightweight predictor trained once on workflow transcripts emits a per-request mask in one pass. It beats static pruning and merging baselines, generalizes to unseen workflows, and its margin is largest at high sparsity. Open: expert swap latency across batches, mid-trajectory drift, and no stated gap to the full model.

  2. 2026-11-19R157Self-Disagreement: training models to hold a correct answer under pushbackModels give up correct answers under wrong counter-arguments, and accuracy training, self-reflection, voting and SCoRe don't fix it: holding vs yielding is a separate 'dialectical competence'. The model states an answer, writes the strongest case against it without the reference, rules on it, and is rewarded with RLVR, the same exact-match check scoring the ruling (no second model, reward model or labels). Capitulation falls 25.3 pp (Qwen3-8B) and 36.1 pp (Gemma-2 9B) vs eleven frontier challengers; Gemma 3 12B from 20.2% to 2.6%; first-answer accuracy holds. Limits: checkable answers only, single-round, and acceptance of correct challenges drops too.

  6. 2026-11-11R033Lazarus: backdooring AI agents by editing a few dormant neuronsRewiring fewer than ten rarely-firing neurons in the final feed-forward block, in closed form and with inference-only compute, makes an open-weight model emit a shell payload (one neuron per token) when a trigger string appears anywhere in context, while passing standard evals. Nemotron-Terminal-8B: 7 of 12,000+ neurons, fitted on 23 trajectories, fire on at least 98.8% of held-out trigger runs and never on benign ones; Qwen 3.6-27B and Gemma-4-31B-IT equally vulnerable. Detectable: agent-built auditors with no reference model flag 91.4% of edited checkpoints at 9.7% FP. Defenses: diff every checkpoint against its declared base, frontier agents as weight auditors, publisher-only sourcing with hashes and a private registry, trigger red-teaming, and containment (least privilege, sandboxed execution, egress controls). Caveats: no audit proves safety, internal models equally exposed via write access, vendor product framing, no observed exploitation.

  7. 2026-11-11R029Synthetic enterprise tenants for evaluating CopilotTo evaluate an assistant over a company's mail, meetings and documents without customer data, the platform generates whole synthetic organizations (pharma, capital markets, IT/software; hundreds of users each) grounded in O*NET, job postings and SEC filings, with histories that evolve over weeks so time-relative queries are testable. A six-stage pipeline turns generic workplace tasks into industry-specific queries grounded in the tenant and validated for accuracy and uniqueness; a quality framework scores structural fidelity, contextual relevance, behavioral realism and content safety; the same config regenerates an identical dataset. Reported: +39% on an internal composite realism metric, some tenants above 0.9, days to automated runs, about $100K saved per dataset, adopted as Copilot's first standardized evaluation baseline. Caveats: no evidence that synthetic scores predict real-customer quality, self-graded realism, AI-generated tests of AI, clean-by-construction data, three industries, single-turn so far, nothing released.

  8. 2026-11-11R025LongCapHarness: a written harness instead of long-context trainingA strong model (GPT-5.6-Sol) splits each long-context task type into capability primitives (code for rules like lookup, counting and ordering; bounded LLM calls for judgements), writes control logic and state that live outside the model's context, and refines them by recursive self-improvement on a dev set; the frozen harness library then runs a small model with an 8K or 32K window. HELMET plus RULER: Llama3-8B 15.14 to 79.32 (+64.18) with no training, above NExtLong-128K (70.15); transfers to Qwen2.5-3B (78.73 vs PolicyLong 64.10); about $541 in API tokens vs $1.9K to $3.7K of GPU time. Caveats: the dev split covers every evaluated task type, perfect Recall comes from code, ICL/RAG/LongQA barely move, small models cannot build their own harness, and inference input tokens roughly double.
