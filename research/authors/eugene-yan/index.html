  1. 2026-11-24R287Twelve public evals, audited: the grader is now the weakest linkA teardown of twelve public agent benchmarks — four on coding and science, six on knowledge work, two on browsing and computer use — arguing that in eight of the twelve there is evidence the grader failed work that should have passed. The per-benchmark numbers are the substance: Terminal Bench 4.0 graded 41 of 65 tasks on unstated requirements, and making those requirements explicit moved one model from 63% to 89%, a swing larger than most model-to-model gaps; DeepSWE marked correct solutions as failures in 23 of 113 tasks; BrowseComp's human trainers disagreed with the reference on 14% of solved questions. False positives are as bad: on one legal benchmark, an external review found material hallucinations in more than 60% of deliverables that passed every criterion, because the judge never sees the source files. Two measurement arguments are worth adopting: average pass rate hides reliability (two agents at 70% can be 70% and 17% under pass^k), and the scarce resource is human review time, not tokens. Key caveats: the eight-of-twelve headline is synthesised from third-party audits and issue trackers without naming which eight, the “hot takes” section mixes evidence with opinion, and a few figures are internally inconsistent.
