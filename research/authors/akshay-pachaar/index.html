  1. 2026-11-25R294When the limit moves from VRAM to SSD: checking an SSD-expert-streaming claim against the project's own docsA claim that local inference has changed shape — that <strong>SSD expert streaming</strong> moves the binding constraint from VRAM capacity to SSD bandwidth — checked against the named project's own published pages. The argued mechanism is coherent and worth keeping on its own: sparse mixture-of-experts models activate a small fraction of their weights per token, so keep attention and the session's hottest experts in fast memory, leave the rest on NVMe, and read an expert only when the router selects it. The four obstacles the post names are the right four. An eviction policy blind to the router's near-future selections evicts exactly what the next tokens need, since expert selection is strongly skewed and autocorrelated across adjacent tokens — which is also what makes prefetch possible. Serialised layer-wise reads leave GPU and storage alternately idle, so device throughput is irrelevant when the pipeline alternates. Speculative decoding verifies several positions at once and widens the per-step expert working set, and it is not optional, because speculation is table stakes for local throughput. And a long prefill sweeps every layer's experts and flushes the decode working set, leaving a cold cache exactly when the user starts waiting — the one most likely to bite in agent workloads, where prompts are long and decode bursts are short. <strong>But the project's own documentation does not claim the feature.</strong> No published page mentions SSD, NVMe, disk offloading, streaming weights from storage, expert reuse ranking, prefetch, flash wear or the prefill/decode interaction; there is no changelog, so the "launching today" framing cannot be dated. The memory language points the other way — use the fit assessment, close memory-heavy applications if loading fails, only variants that fit with room to work are recommended — which is fit-or-refuse, not streaming. The demo model is listed in the project's own catalog at 180B total and 6B active, not the 300B the post states, its 4-bit download is 115 GB, it publishes no tokens-per-second figure, and it is absent from the benchmark tables. What is solid: Apache-2.0, fully local, per-chip kernel tuning at download time, pre-download throughput estimates, desktop app and CLI across three platforms, and published benchmarks of 1.32x prefill and 2.19x decode against a popular local runner on a 48 GB Mac and 1.34x / 2.17x on a developer box, across 11 models at 64k context — with the project's own creditable note that some of the Mac decode gain "partly compares speculative decoding against almost none". Two readings survive and the published evidence cannot separate them: the feature is new and wholly undocumented, or the post describes a capability the project does not have. The parameter error is not cosmetic, since the whole argument is about fitting a model that should not fit.
