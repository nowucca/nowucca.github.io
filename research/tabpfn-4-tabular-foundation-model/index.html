TabPFN-4: a tabular foundation model that scales in-context prediction to 10 million rows

Sources: TabPFN-4: The next step for predictive intelligence (Prior Labs technical report preview, 2026-11-19); announcement by Frank Hutter, Prior Labs co-founder. Model announced as finished training; availability via waitlist.

What a tabular foundation model does

Instead of training a model per dataset, a tabular foundation model is pretrained once and predicts by attending over the whole training table in context, in a single forward pass. That is why it needs no tuning, and also why it was hard to scale: attention over rows grows quadratically. The previous generation (TabPFN v2, published in Nature) was limited to about 10,000 rows.

What changed

TabPFN-4 uses a new sub-quadratic architecture. The report's scaling claim: a 10 million row by 500 column table predicted in 38 minutes on a single RTX PRO 6000, against extrapolated estimates of about 65 hours for TabPFN-3.5 and 127 hours for Kumo-Tabular L (which ran out of GPU memory above 262k rows). Speed-ups grow with table size.

Gains are largest on temporal and grouped data, where rows are not independent draws; classical models handle that only through feature engineering. On 38 historical Kaggle competitions with default settings, it would have tied or beaten the original winner in 14, including 7 of 13 prize-money competitions.

How to read the claims

  • Vendor report on vendor-chosen benchmarks, several of them recent; independent replication is not yet available.

  • "Moves the Pareto frontier" means accuracy versus inference time, not top Elo everywhere: on TabArena it trails one baseline on raw score.

  • Extrapolated baselines. The 65 h and 127 h figures are extrapolations, not measurements.

  • Kaggle comparisons are retrospective against private leaderboards, without the time pressure or leakage dynamics of live competitions.

  • Not yet available, so latency, cost and licensing terms for production use are unknown.

Tradeoffs

  • No-tuning baseline vs. control. A single forward pass replaces feature engineering and hyperparameter search, at the cost of interpretability and a large GPU at inference.

  • Inference-heavy. Every prediction re-reads the training table; cost moves from training time to prediction time.

  • Data residency. An API default means sending tables to a provider unless a local option exists.

Review checklist before adopting

  • Benchmark against your tuned gradient-boosting baseline on your own temporal splits.

  • Measure prediction latency and GPU cost at your table size, not the report's.

  • Check behaviour under distribution shift and with grouped entities.

  • Confirm licensing and whether a local deployment exists for sensitive data.

  • Keep a simple baseline in production for comparison and fallback.
