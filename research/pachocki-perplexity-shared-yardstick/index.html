Source: Jakub Pachocki (OpenAI Chief Scientist), "Perplexity and Intelligence" (2026-11-22). Found via TLDR AI 2026-11-23.

The argument

Pachocki argues that most of what models can do comes from pretraining, a compressor of text, and that post-training mostly draws out what is already there. If so, held-out perplexity (equivalently, cross-entropy loss) is the most direct single measure of underlying capability, and labs should report it on a common basis. OpenAI publishes its own internal numbers to start that.

The ladder he gives

In log-perplexity on OpenAI's held-out set:

  • GPT-2: coherent paragraphs.

  • GPT-3: in-context learning.

  • GPT-3.5, about 0.99: fluent chat.

  • GPT-4, about 0.91: chain-of-thought becomes productive.

  • Below 0.8: emergent cybersecurity and mathematical capability.

  • About 0.76: a model began producing results on open math problems.

  • He expects "transformative capabilities" around 0.68 to 0.7, and says a similar run of pretraining advances could arrive as soon as 2027.

Policy proposal

Shared "common limits" tied to this metric: thresholds at which labs agree to extra safeguards, instead of each lab picking its own evals.

Caveats he lists

Perplexity is hard to compare across labs (different tokenisers and held-out data), it is detached from task evals, reasoning at inference does not lower it, and the floor set by the entropy of text is unknown.

How to read it

  • It is a position essay from a lab, framed in its own interest: a metric OpenAI already reports well on, and norms that suit it. The 0.76 open-math claim and the 2027 timeline are assertions without published evidence.

  • It cuts against the agent-centred view in R194 and R280. METR and Naam measure what models do on long tasks; Pachocki says watch the pretraining loss, because capability follows it. The disagreement is what to measure, not just the numbers.

  • For a staff engineer: perplexity thresholds will not tell you whether a model can do your task. Keep evaluating on your own workloads. The practical signal is that OpenAI expects step changes from pretraining, not only from post-training and harnesses, over the next year.
