What it is

Cognition's in-house coding and agent model, trained with reinforcement learning only on top of Moonshot's open-weight Kimi K3. The post names K3 as the base but compares against K3.1, so the exact base is ambiguous. RL environments were widened to short and long tasks in science, performance engineering and security. Post-training was changed to control rollout length, because swings in length had destabilised earlier runs. Training ran stably for days on tasks lasting minutes to hours.

Security: ExploitBench 80.0 (SWE-2: 36.1; Opus 5.5: 91; GPT-6.2 Sol: 100), CWE-Bench v2 65.8. Exploit-solving rate grows log-linearly with rollout time from 3 minutes to 6 hours.

Ultrafast: the same model on Cerebras at about 10x the speed, roughly 600 tokens per second against 60.

Availability: Devin Desktop, Cloud and CLI; free on Desktop and CLI through December 8, then discounted. A more permissive SWE-2.1 Cyber goes only to Devin Security. A canary sandbox-escape eval found new sandbox vulnerabilities four times during training.

Caveats

Scores use a simulated clock (1 second to first token plus a per-token cost), so speed does not affect them. Harnesses differ: Devin CLI for open models, each lab's preferred harness for closed ones. ExploitBench takes the best of five runs. Pricing is not given.

Why it matters for a staff engineer

This is the clearest current example of a product company buying frontier-adjacent coding ability by running RL on an open base, then making it cheap and fast with specialised inference hardware. It roughly doubles its predecessor but still trails the three frontier labs on most benchmarks, so the pitch is cost and speed, not peak quality. The sandbox-escape findings during training are worth noting for anyone running RL or long agent loops: assume a capable agent will probe its sandbox, and test containment as part of training, not after.
