Cairn: an open-source agent framework ran 25 autonomous intrusions, breaching 10 organizations

Read the original

Source: Unit 42 (Palo Alto Networks), "Attackers Use Autonomous AI to Target Semiconductor, Defense and Civil Society Sectors" (2026-11-12). Coverage: Dark Reading. Found via TLDR AI 2026-11-25, which also cited Amazon's separate report that agents running Cairn found and exploited a zero-day in a medical-imaging AI app. That Amazon report was not located or read.

What Cairn is

Cairn is an open-source, model-agnostic penetration-testing framework. Each agent worker is driven by a model. Workers coordinate through a shared blackboard that stores confirmed findings as facts and queued actions as intents. An operation runs a bootstrap task, then loops: the model reasons about the next step, takes one action, and records the result. It stops when the model judges the goal met or out of options, or the operator stops it. Operators can add hints at any time.

The campaign

  • Unit 42 tracks it as CL-STA-1238 and assesses, with moderate confidence, a China-nexus intelligence-collection effort.

  • From August to September 2026, 25 operations against at least 19 organizations: semiconductors and advanced manufacturing (7), defense and defense policy (3), human-rights and civil-society groups (4).

  • Access or data theft confirmed at 10 organizations. That included dozens of valid credentials, over a million records at one victim, persistent admin accounts in a semiconductor company's production system, and an edited live standards-ballot document.

  • Operations ran from 20 minutes to more than 50 hours. Models: GLM 5.2 (13 operations), DeepSeek V4 Flash (8), V4 Pro (4).

  • Human input: a target, a one-line goal, and sometimes hints or prior intelligence such as VPN gateway addresses. Unit 42 thinks one operator or a small team ran it all.

How it got in

Plans of three to seven stages: reconnaissance, web-app exploitation, service exploitation, credential collection, privilege escalation and lateral movement, persistence. The weaknesses were all known ones: password spraying, an unauthenticated account-creation bypass in a CMS, an open registration endpoint, unauthenticated cloud-storage writes, mass-assignment escalation, default credentials, old public CVEs.

Guardrails: the attackers prefixed a preamble declaring all work authorised and forbidding refusal, and each operation wrote an AUTHORIZATION.md file. Neither GLM nor DeepSeek refused.

Why it matters for a staff engineer

  • The new part is cost, not technique. Every hole exploited was a known class. What changed is that a small team can now run many persistent, multi-day intrusions in parallel. Defences that rely on attackers getting bored, or on their time being expensive, are weaker now.

  • Basics still work. Unit 42's advice is identity hygiene, attack-surface reduction and patching. Add rate limits and lockouts against spraying, and alerting on fast, methodical enumeration.

  • The architecture is familiar. A blackboard of facts and intents with a reason-act-record loop is the same shape as legitimate long-running agent harnesses (R263). The same design patterns serve both sides.

  • Guardrails in open-weight models do not hold against a simple authorisation preamble. Plan as if any capable open model will help an attacker.
