Adversarial distillation: how OpenAI protects reasoning traces, and what it may mean for open weights

OpenAI, 2026-11-20; shared by Ethan MollickPublished 2026-11-20Summarized 2026-11-20

Read the original

securitydistillationopen-weightspolicy

Sources: OpenAI, Combatting adversarial distillation, 2026-11-20. Context from Ethan Mollick's post the same day, which suggests the end of easy distillation may explain why open-weight progress has slowed.

The claim

Distillation trains one model on another's outputs. OpenAI frames unauthorized distillation as a safety problem as well as an IP problem: a student model can acquire a frontier model's capabilities, in cybersecurity for example, without the safeguards that limit how the original uses them. Its defences focus on the model's protected reasoning, the full internal chain of thought, because that carries more transferable skill than final answers or reasoning summaries.

Evidence on what transfers

In an internal study, the same pretrained student was fine-tuned on trajectories from frontier-level teachers working on coding and defensive security tasks. One run used what users normally see (answers, reasoning summaries, tool calls). The other added the exact reasoning traces. The student trained on exact traces generally scored higher on two offensive-cyber benchmarks after fine-tuning. After a light round of RL it still led at the largest inference budget, while the summary-trained student led at smaller budgets. The takeaway: hiding exact traces limits capability transfer even when every individual task was allowed.

The defence stack

Per request

  • Adversarial training, so the model refuses to disclose protected reasoning while still explaining its answers.

  • Input classifiers for extraction attempts, and output classifiers for leaks, both in assistant messages and in tool-call arguments. A model can leak through a tool argument without the leak appearing in its answer.

  • API controls on reuse. Encrypted reasoning is tied to the conversation that produced it, and it is dropped if an earlier message is rewritten. Older model families cannot read newer models' reasoning, and the boundaries between users, workspaces and organizations were tightened after a September disclosure about moving encrypted reasoning between conversations.

Per campaign

  • Investigating coordinated account networks, enforcing against the whole network, and sharing indicators with other providers. Weekly enforcement ranges from thousands to tens of thousands of accounts and rose sharply over the summer.

  • Attacks found in investigations feed back into training and evaluation data.

Reported results

In adaptive black-box red-teaming across six attacker models, the attack success rate fell sharply:

AttackGPT-6 Astra, original safeguardsGPT-6.2 Astra, updated safeguards
Extract reasoning from an earlier request54.0%0.2%
Extract reasoning from the current request22.9%0.9%

The post names DeepSeek and individuals linked to Moonshot AI in earlier campaigns. It also states support for US legislation on export controls and on sanctioning illicit extraction.

The open-weights question

If open-weight labs relied on frontier outputs, especially reasoning traces, to close the gap, tighter defences at OpenAI and Anthropic would slow them. That is a hypothesis, not a finding: the post shows that defences work against extraction, not how much open models depended on it. Other explanations for a slowdown (compute limits, data, talent, release timing) remain open. Watch whether open releases over the coming months narrow or widen the gap on reasoning-heavy benchmarks.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

  • Self-reported evaluation. The red-team numbers come from the defender's own harness.

  • Legitimate use. Classifiers that block extraction can also refuse genuine requests to explain an answer, and the post does not report a false-positive rate.

  • Summaries still transfer. Distillation from visible outputs remains possible and is legitimate in many settings. Protection reduces the value of what an attacker can extract; it does not prevent distillation.

  • Policy entanglement. Safety, competition and national-security arguments are mixed together, so read the technical claims separately from the advocacy.

Checklist for teams exposing reasoning models through an API or agent

  • Do your output filters cover tool-call arguments, not only assistant text?

  • Is any hidden state (reasoning, memory) bound to its originating session and tenant?

  • Are previously successful extraction prompts kept as regression tests?

  • Do you monitor for coordinated extraction across accounts, not only single bad requests?

  • If you distill from a vendor model, do the vendor's terms allow it?
