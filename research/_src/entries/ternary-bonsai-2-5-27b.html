What it is

A low-bit ("ternary") version of Qwen3.8 27B that fits in 5.9 GB, small enough for a laptop GPU or a recent iPhone. Version 2.5 targets on-device agent work: long chains of tool calls, recovering when a step fails, and less looping and over-thinking than version 2. Weights are on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0; it runs on llama.cpp (NVIDIA, Intel) and MLX (Mac, iPhone, iPad).

The biggest jumps from version 2 are on multi-step agent benchmarks (AppWorld 51 to 90, DeepSWE 0 to 27). The remaining gap to full precision is largest on Terminal-Bench (about 11 points). Speed: 291 tokens per second on an H100 and 204 on an RTX 4090 with multi-token prediction.

Caveats

Comparisons are only against its own base and predecessor, not against other small models. No harness, context or sampling settings. The "strongest model of its size" claim is not backed on the page.

Why it matters for a staff engineer

Extreme quantisation used to cost the most on exactly the skills agents need: long tool-call chains and error recovery (Bonsai 2 scored 0 on DeepSWE). Version 2.5 suggests that gap is mostly a training problem, closable with agent-targeted post-training of the quantised model. A 27B model at 5.9 GB that keeps most of its agentic ability makes local agents on private files practical, which matters for privacy-sensitive work and offline use. Verify on your own tasks first; the benchmark set is self-chosen. Compare R197 (Inkling 2 Mini, a 26B model built to run locally) and R300 (shrinking the memory for context rather than weights).
