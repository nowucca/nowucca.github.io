Status: claim, not result. As of 2026-11-20 no independent expert has confirmed the Lean development. Treat everything below as a description of what is claimed and how one would check it.

What is claimed

Alpöge, a number theorist at Anthropic and a former Harvard Society of Fellows junior fellow, says that working with Claude he found a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, and that the argument is formalized in Lean with no added axioms or assumptions. He expects the Extended Riemann Hypothesis to follow, and expects large simplifications soon.

He released only the Lean files. His stated reason is that a human-readable write-up generated from the formal proof would run to hundreds of thousands of pages and would not be worth reading, so the checker output is the artifact.

The mathematical idea, as described

The note frames the method as an analogue of Stepanov's polynomial multiplicity method (the elementary route to the Riemann Hypothesis for curves over finite fields). Instead of auxiliary polynomials vanishing to high order, it chains theta lifts, with a chain of length about the square root of X used to control the von Mangoldt sum of length X. The note is a sketch; the detail lives only in Lean.

The stated timeline runs from early October 2026 to GRH on 16 November, about six weeks of work with the model.

Why this matters to engineers, whether or not it holds

  1. The proof object shifts from prose to a checked artifact. If mathematicians accept a result whose only full form is machine-checked Lean, the review question becomes "is the statement right and is the kernel trusted", not "is every step convincing". That is the same shift code review faces with agent-written code plus proofs or strong oracles.

  2. Readability is no longer guaranteed. A model can produce a correct argument far larger than any human will read. Trust then rests on the checker, the statement, and the axioms, which is a smaller and more auditable base.

  3. Verification is the bottleneck. The production side took weeks. Expert checking of the statement and the build may take longer.

How a Lean proof claim fails (review checklist)

A Lean file that compiles can still prove the wrong thing. Before calling it verified, check:

  • Statement fidelity. Does the top-level theorem state RH as mathematicians mean it (all nontrivial zeros of ζ on Re s = 1/2), using Mathlib's standard definitions rather than locally redefined ones? A subtly different definition of zeta, of "nontrivial zero", or of the critical strip is the most likely failure.

  • Axioms. Run #print axioms on the main theorem. Only the standard three (propositional extensionality, Classical.choice, Quot.sound) should appear. Any sorry, custom axiom, or opaque constant with assumed properties breaks the claim.

  • Kernel trust. Recheck with an independent checker (for example lean4checker or an external type checker) to rule out elaborator or tactic bugs, implemented_by, native_decide or other code-execution escapes.

  • Build reproducibility. Pinned Lean and Mathlib versions, a clean build from the tarball, and a hash of what was checked.

  • Size and structure. A very large development should be checked for definitions that quietly assume the conclusion, such as hypotheses smuggled into structure fields.

  • Human extraction. Even if the proof is correct, a shorter human-readable argument is what the field will want. The promised simplifications are the second test.

Failure modes to keep in view

  • Correct proof of a different statement. The classic formalization failure, and the one a non-expert reading the note cannot rule out.

  • Trusted-base escape. Tactics or compiler hooks that let the kernel accept something it never checked.

  • Social proof as verification. Reshares from well-known accounts are not review. The right signal is a named expert or an independent Lean team confirming the statement and axioms.

  • Timing incentives. Large announcements near financial events invite scepticism. The only answer is independent checking.

What to watch

  • Independent reports that the tarball builds and that #print axioms is clean.

  • Experts in analytic number theory or the Lean community commenting on the top-level statement.

  • The promised simplifications and an Extended RH follow-up.
