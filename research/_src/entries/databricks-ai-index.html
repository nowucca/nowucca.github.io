Source: Databricks, Introducing the Databricks AI Index: The state of enterprise AI tokenomics (2026-11-16). Shared on LinkedIn by Databricks CEO Ali Ghodsi.

What it is

A public, weekly-updated index built from two sources: aggregated, anonymized traffic from about 10,000 companies routing models through Databricks' gateway (including traffic to external providers), and an internal coding-agent benchmark built from real Databricks engineering workflows (PRs, reviews, investigations) with private-codebase tasks and withheld tests to limit contamination. It reports token and spend share by model and provider, per-user spending, and success rate and cost per completed task for model-plus-harness combinations (Claude Code and Codex, models from nine labs).

Headline findings (as reported)

  • Launch cadence. Since May 2026 a new flagship model has shipped about weekly, every 3.7 days counting regional labs.

  • Share moves fast with quality. Claude Opus 5.5 went from 7.2% to 37.9% of proprietary-model tokens in six weeks, lifting Anthropic from 57.6% to 68.8%, after a period where OpenAI's GPT-6 Sol and Astra had gained share.

  • Multi-provider is now normal. Enterprises using a single provider fell from about 64% in July to 28% by November 2.

  • Open-weight models quadrupled. From 7.3% of enterprise tokens in July to 30.9% in November. Three open-weight models are in the coding benchmark's top 10; DeepSeek V4.1 and GLM 5.3 tie for third at 93.1%.

  • Token price is not task cost. GPT-6 Luna and Claude Opus 5.5 both hit 89.1% success, at about $0.05 vs $1.62 per task (roughly 32x). Gemini 3.8 Flash has a low token price but averaged 140 turns per task and cost $11.97 per task, among the most expensive.

  • The top model is rarely the best value. Claude Fable 5.1 led at 96.0% for $7.47 per task; Muse Wonder scored 94.9% for $1.53. Fable 5.1's proprietary-token share fell from 5.5% to 0.6% in six weeks.

  • Spend is concentrated. Median user spend was $2.49 vs $699.74 at the 99th percentile (about 281x). Median weekly spend more than doubled over six weeks while the tail stayed flat.

  • Internal practice. Databricks replays its own traces against the cost-quality frontier before upgrading: Opus 5.5 gave better quality at 29% lower cost per session than its predecessor and became the default, while an earlier Opus 4.8 to Opus 5 upgrade was judged not worth the cost.

What it means for engineering teams

  • Measure cost per completed task, not per token. Turn count, output length and retries dominate; a cheap model that loops is expensive.

  • Evaluate model and harness together. The same model ranks differently under different harnesses, and five models from four labs sit on the frontier.

  • Build for switching. With weekly launches and share swinging 30 points in weeks, a gateway or abstraction that lets you swap models per task is cheaper than re-platforming.

  • Budget for the tail. A few heavy agent users drive most spend; per-user visibility matters more than average cost.

Caveats

  • Vendor data from the vendor's own gateway customers, who skew toward multi-provider routing by construction.

  • The coding benchmark is built on Databricks' own codebase and workflows; results may not transfer.

  • The CEO's LinkedIn summary phrases provider counts differently (75% with two or more, 50% with three) from the blog's single-provider figure.

  • The post promotes the vendor's gateway and routing products.

Review checklist

  • Do you track success rate and cost per completed task for each model and harness you use?

  • Can you replay your own traces against a new model before switching the default?

  • Is model choice configurable per task type, with a cheaper default for routine work?

  • Do you have per-user and per-agent spend visibility, with alerts on the tail?
