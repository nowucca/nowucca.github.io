Source: Hrishikesh Kamath, "I Analyzed the AI Stack Used by 40,000+ Companies" (2026-11-22), part 2 of a series. Found via TLDR Dev 2026-11-23. Most of the rankings appear only in chart images, so what follows keeps to the numbers stated in the post's text.

Method

The data appears to come from public subprocessor and vendor disclosure lists of about 40,000 companies; the post does not say how the companies were chosen. Growth is the one-month net change (16 October to 13 November 2026) in how many companies name a vendor. The author sorted vendors into 20 categories by hand, and a vendor can sit in more than one. "Model provider" covers labs, routers, cloud model services and GPU compute.

Findings

  • Multi-provider is the norm. 44.8% of companies that name a model provider name two or more.

  • Anthropic and OpenAI lead the provider ranking. AWS Bedrock and Azure OpenAI show up as cloud model services.

  • Routers and gateways grow fastest among provider types, mostly OpenRouter. Inference and GPU providers are on slightly more lists than routers. The author reads both as a sign of open-weight adoption.

  • Chinese models: a small share name one directly, DeepSeek first. They grow about as fast as everything else, and most companies that name one also name a US provider.

  • Fastest-growing categories: agent sandboxes, web search APIs, agent frameworks and connectors, model routers, web scraping for agents, voice generation, LLM observability and evals, meeting notetakers, inference and GPU compute, speech-to-text.

  • Search APIs: older ones (SerpApi, Serper) keep growing alongside agent-native search (Exa, Parallel, Tavily).

Limits

  • Disclosures show who a company names, not what it runs. Models behind a router, self-hosted models and open models rented on GPU clouds are undercounted.

  • One month of growth data, no denominators for most percentages, and subjective categories.

  • An OpenRouter figure of 73% open-weight tokens is from an embedded tweet about OpenRouter's own traffic, not from this dataset.

Why it matters for a staff engineer

Subprocessor lists are a cheap, public signal for what the market is wiring together, and this one says the growth is in the layer around the model: sandboxes, search, scraping, routing and observability. That matches the Databricks enterprise index (R116) on multi-provider use. For a platform team, the 44.8% multi-provider figure is the practical one: provider abstraction and routing are now normal, not an edge case. Treat the shares as share of disclosing companies, not market share.
