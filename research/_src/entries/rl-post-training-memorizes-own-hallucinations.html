Source: Atmakuru and Goyal (Cornell), "LLMs Memorize Their Own Hallucinations During Post-Training", arXiv 2611.26863, 19 Nov 2026. Surfaced by DAIR.AI.

The claim

Frontier post-training recipes were tuned on math and code, then applied everywhere. Two pieces of those recipes, outcome-based RL rewards and reasoning chains that propose and backtrack hypotheses, may do harm on knowledge tasks: the model invents facts inside its long reasoning, the final answer is rewarded, and the invented facts get reinforced along with it.

Method

  • Train six open models (two families, two architectures) with a standard post-training pipeline on knowledge tasks.

  • During RL, extract each model's self-generated hallucinations from its reasoning chains.

  • Track how well the model recalls each hallucinated "fact" for the rest of training, split by whether that rollout was rewarded.

Findings

  • Rewarded hallucinations stick. Recall of hallucinations in rewarded rollouts rose 4.83% on average, against 0.17% for hallucinations that got no training signal.

  • Retracting doesn't help. Models memorised false facts they proposed and then explicitly retracted in the same chain. The propose-and-backtrack template inherited from reasoning training brings no benefit to knowledge recall.

  • Hardest questions are worst. Memorisation was strongest on the hardest training questions, where the model guesses most.

  • Less abstention. Rewarding a hallucination made the model 7.23% less likely to abstain on it later.

Why it matters

Outcome-only rewards don't inspect the chain. If the answer is right for the wrong reasons, or the chain contains confident fabrications on the way, the gradient pushes all of it up. For knowledge-heavy deployments this is a quiet source of confident errors that no answer-level eval catches.

Tradeoffs and limits

  • Open models and a standard pipeline; frontier recipes with process supervision or chain filtering may behave differently.

  • Effect sizes are averages of a few percent; the practical impact depends on how often hard knowledge questions appear in RL data.

  • The abstract doesn't report mitigations; any fix (chain filtering, process rewards, abstention rewards) needs its own evaluation.

Failure modes to watch

  • Using math-style outcome rewards on factual QA without checking the reasoning.

  • Keeping long proposal-and-backtrack chains for tasks where they add no accuracy.

  • Measuring only final-answer accuracy after RL, so a drop in abstention or a rise in confident fabrication goes unseen.

  • Concentrating RL on hard questions where the model mostly guesses.

Review checklist

  • For knowledge tasks, are reasoning chains checked or filtered for fabricated claims before reward?

  • Is abstention rate tracked before and after RL, alongside accuracy?

  • Is there a held-out probe of facts the model invented during training?

  • Does the recipe justify long reasoning chains for knowledge recall, or just inherit them?

  • Are the hardest, most-guessed questions down-weighted or given an explicit abstain reward?
