Ye, Lou, Sun, Song, Yan, Kasriel, Zhang, He, Song, Yang and Kong (Caltech, Stanford, UC Berkeley, Apodex), arXiv 2608.13522; code at sunblaze-ucb/vero. Found via Daniel Vaughan's blog.

Vero sets the cost baseline for the strong end of formal methods: agents writing code plus Lean proofs for whole multi-module repositories. 43 Lean 4 repositories (translated from Python, Dafny, Verus and Coq sources) carry 743 scored APIs and 2,705 specs. An instance counts only if every spec is proved.

Mechanism. Two modes: code-and-proof (agent writes implementations and proofs) and proof-only (reference code given). Agents can submit machine-checked evidence that a spec is unsatisfiable or a reference is wrong, which audits the benchmark itself. Edits are limited to marked regions, axioms are allowlisted, and trivializing declarations are screened out. Harnesses were Codex and Claude Code with a 90-minute budget per run.

Results. Fully solved, code-and-proof / proof-only: GPT-5.5 xhigh 27 and 25 of 43; Claude Opus 4.8 8 and 10; GPT-5.5 medium 2 and 6; Claude Sonnet 5 2 and 2. Ten instances beat every configuration. GPT-5.5 xhigh proves 87% of individual specs, but only 51% of deep ones (helper depth 4 or more). Total cost across 43 instances: about $2,900 for GPT-5.5 xhigh, $2,000 to $2,300 for Opus 4.8, $630 to $790 for Sonnet 5.

Why it matters for efficiency. It prices the trade: roughly $67 per repository at the top setting, with no human proof review afterward. Solve rate rose much faster than spend: GPT-5.5 xhigh cost about three times medium and solved over ten times as many instances, so cheap settings are not a bargain.

Caveats. Lean only, and the corpus favors code that translates cleanly, so no concurrency or temporal protocols. No maintenance tasks (re-proving after a change). A satisfiable spec is not a correct spec, so specs were reviewed by hand. The blog's language-by-language success table and its "3 to 10 times tokens" figure are not in the paper.
