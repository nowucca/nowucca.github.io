Four AI builds that move learning outcomes, and how models fail at them

Read the original

Authors Dr Philippa Hardman

teachinglearning-designai-literacyverificationmodel-comparison

Dr Philippa Hardman matched the learning-science evidence to what coding models can now build. Four interactive formats beat the quiz: simulations (g = 0.85), self-explanation with AI marking (g = 0.55), contrasting cases (d = 0.50) and faded worked examples (g = 0.48). By comparison, classroom quizzes come in at 0.37. In her eight tests of Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.2 Astra and Gemini 4 Argon, the main failure was the "answer leak": pages that work and look polished but do the learner's thinking for them (Dr Phil's Newsletter, 19 Nov 2026).

The four builds

Simulationg = 0.85 (Chernikova et al., 2020)The learner must make a judgement that depends on several conditions, and practising for real is riskyThere is no decision to make

Support must fit the learner: novices need worked examples before simulations, and experts need reflection.

The method: research, then build

  1. Objective. What must people be able to do afterwards?

  2. Research. Which interaction does the evidence say builds that ability?

  3. Brief. In a two-prompt pattern, first have the model list every rule in the source document with page numbers and build nothing. Then have it build from only those facts. Say what must stay hidden until the learner commits. Tell it to stop and ask when instructions conflict.

  4. Check. Walk the build as a learner who knows nothing, looking for leaks and for contradictions with the source.

What the model tests showed

  • Strong at rules. Claude and ChatGPT got scoring and threshold logic right first time.

  • Mostly faithful to the source. Fact lists were correct in seven of eight tests. In the eighth, Gemini added a detail that isn't in the guide.

  • Weak at AI inside the page. Marking free text needs the page to call a model. Claude (via artifacts) and ChatGPT got it working; Gemini declined.

  • Silent changes. All three made changes they weren't asked for, and none flagged them. ChatGPT added a question, Claude left out a ladder type, and Gemini pulled in outside libraries.

  • They follow your mistakes. In her first three tests, all nine builds leaked the answer, largely because a brief asked for a revealing label.

  • Wrong but plausible. A Gemini simulation blamed a "steep angle" for a ladder that was too shallow, and its marking praised a wrong answer.

Lessons that carry beyond L&D

  • Polished output hides spec violations. Check against the source, not the look.

  • "List the facts first, then build only from them" is a cheap way to reduce invented detail.

  • Ask the model to list what it couldn't do as asked. Unflagged deviations were the norm.

  • The bottleneck moves from building to knowing what should be built and checking it.

Review checklist for an AI-built interactive

  • Does any control, label or default reveal the answer before the learner commits?

  • Does every rule shown match the source document?

  • Are invented details labeled?

  • Does AI marking fail safe, saying when it can't judge?

  • Is the format the one the learning goal calls for, not the impressive one?
