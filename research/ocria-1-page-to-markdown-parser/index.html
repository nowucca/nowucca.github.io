OCRia-1: a 4B page-to-Markdown parser trained with verifiable rewards

What it is

OCRia-1 is a 4B vision-language model, fine-tuned from Qwen3.5-4B, that takes one page image and writes the whole page as Markdown, with tables as HTML. There is no layout detector, no separate OCR engine and no PDF text layer in the loop: the text comes from the image alone, in one call per page. Layout boxes (for visual grounding) are added afterwards by separate code in the repo, using layout regions, PDF word positions or attention.

It targets the cases document parsers most often get wrong:

  • Tables: nested and spanning headers become colspan and rowspan; irregular grids and very long financial tables are kept intact.

  • Charts: every chart becomes an HTML table of its data points, with each value under its series and label.

  • Meaningful formatting: strikethrough, underline, highlight, sub and superscript, checkboxes and blank form fields are kept, because in contracts, hearing notices and forms they carry meaning.

  • Math: formulas in LaTeX, including handwritten ones.

  • Page structure: headers, footers, page numbers and watermarks go in their own tags; multi-column text is unrolled; diagrams come out as Mermaid.

The same model, quantized and served with a speculative decoder, powers LlamaParse, the company's hosted parser, which also handles multi-page documents.

The largest gap is on charts: 82.5 on ParseBench 1 charts against 69.1 for the next open-weight model, and several strong parsers score near zero there because they do not extract chart data at all. On plain text and content faithfulness the field is bunched within a few points.

Three caveats the card states itself, which matter more than the ranks:

  1. ParseBench is the vendor's own benchmark. The two headline ranks come from a leaderboard LlamaIndex maintains. On the three independent benchmarks the lead is real but small (0.3 to 2 points), within what rendering choices move.

  2. Rendering resolution moves scores. Giving the model 2,451 image tokens per page instead of 1,240 raises its olmOCR-bench score by 2.4 points with no weight change, and other models respond the same way. Comparisons across papers that render differently are not like for like.

  3. Output is post-processed before scoring. The model emits constructs benchmarks do not expect (page-furniture tags, image markers, Mermaid), which are stripped, and markup inside table cells is normalized. Your own pipeline needs the same normalization, or your numbers will differ.

Some comparison rows on the ParseBench 1 table are marked unverified on the leaderboard, and the report's own ParseBench 1 figure (87, four dimensions, no grounding) differs from the card's table.

How it was trained

  • Data: 100,979 pages, about 0.37B target tokens. 82,943 real pages from eleven permissively licensed source families (Internet Archive, arXiv, US and UK government statistics, PubMed Central, contracts with tracked changes, Library of Congress, EUR-Lex, World Bank and others), transcribed by a teacher pipeline and kept only when checked against the page image and the PDF text. 18,036 synthetic pages (mostly tables and charts, plus forms and formatting) rendered in the styles of real documents, so their ground truth is exact. No customer data.

  • SFT: two epochs on pages of about 1,024 px.

  • RL with verifiable rewards (GRPO): rewards score each dimension the way a benchmark would (table structure and cell values, chart data points, text and inline formatting), with penalties for merged or split tables, unfinished tables, repetition and invented formatting.

  • Checkpoint averaging: the release is a uniform average of two RL checkpoints that were strong in different dimensions.

The interesting design choice is the reward: because parsing has a checkable target, the RL stage optimizes the evaluation metric almost directly. That explains strong benchmark numbers, and is also why independent benchmarks matter more than the in-house one.

Running it

The public repo holds unoptimized BF16 weights (10.35 GB). Serve with vLLM 0.30 or later; send each page as one image with the shipped system prompt, no user text, and thinking turned off (the Qwen3.5 template turns thinking on by default, and with it on the model answers in the wrong format). Ollama now carries it as well.

Speed depends heavily on the stack. The hosted LlamaParse stack on one B200 (NVFP4 and FP8 quantization, a 0.69B block-diffusion speculator, custom kernels) decodes about 4,700 tokens per second for one request and parses 10.2 pages per second at 64 concurrent requests. The public weights on stock vLLM decode about 320 to 330 tokens per second per page, roughly nine times slower. Plan capacity from the second number, not the first.

License: open weights, not open source

The weights and grounding code ship under a custom license based on Open RAIL-M:

  • free for research, teaching, evaluation and benchmarking, and internal R&D that does not reach production;

  • free for personal use and for commercial use by organizations under 2M in annual revenue and under 2M in total funding;

  • a commercial license is required above those thresholds, and for any document-parsing, OCR or extraction product that competes with LlamaParse or LlamaCloud, at any size.

Outputs belong to the user. Teams above the thresholds should treat this as an evaluation model or a reason to buy the hosted service, not as a free drop-in.

What to take from it

  • For RAG and agent pipelines, ingestion quality caps retrieval quality. A parser that keeps spanning headers, chart values and strikethroughs removes whole classes of downstream errors (wrong numbers from flattened tables, withdrawn clauses read as live) that no retriever can recover.

  • Image-only parsing is a trade. Skipping the PDF text layer handles scans and handwriting uniformly, but means every character is a model prediction; for born-digital PDFs, compare against the text layer on a sample before trusting numbers in financial tables.

  • Evaluate on your documents at your resolution, with the card's normalization applied, before reading anything into a one-point lead.

  • Check the license before building on it. The competitive-use clause applies regardless of company size.
