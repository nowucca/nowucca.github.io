Harvey's Tenet recipe: RL post-training lifts US open-weight models on legal agent tasks

Source: Harvey Research, "Pushing the US open-weight frontier" (2026-11-24), with Baseten. Found via TLDR AI 2026-11-25. Vendor research: benchmarks are Harvey-run, and the post ends in a demo request.

What they did

Harvey took its "Tenet" post-training recipe and applied it to four recent US open-weight models: Muse Spark Open (Meta), Nemotron 3.5 Super VL (NVIDIA), Beam 1.0 (Reflection) and Inkling 2 Small (Thinking Machines), with GLM-5.3 (Z.ai) for comparison.

  • Method: rank-64 LoRA, the CISPO RL objective with Dr. GRPO-style advantage normalisation, asynchronous RL.

  • Environments: 1,250 agentic legal tasks across 24 practice areas, 8 rollouts per task, up to 200 turns and one hour each.

  • Reward: an LLM judge scoring against expert-written rubrics, with no length penalty.

Results

  • Muse Spark Open: 30.3% all-pass on Harvey's Legal Agent Bench, against 25.8% for the base model at maximum effort, while using 36% fewer output tokens.

  • Nemotron, Beam and Inkling 2 Small each pass 20% after training.

  • Inkling 2 Small on contract tasks: 4.0% to 13.0%. Beam 1.0 on Redline Bench: 40.6% to 51.1%.

  • In a pairwise generative-reward-model tournament, post-trained models were preferred 98% of the time for some models, but there was no significant change for Muse or Inkling.

  • Also reported: PRBench 58.9 to 63.0, APEX-v1 Legal 74.3 to 80.1.

Caveats they state

No closed-model baseline in the post. Hyperparameters differ per model and checkpoints were chosen on a hold-out set. Extraction F1 on CUAD fell 2.4 to 9.4 points for every model, so the recipe trades some precise extraction for agentic task completion. Some gains may come from longer reasoning.

Why it matters for a staff engineer

This is a worked example of the domain-RL playbook: a LoRA on an open base, agentic environments drawn from real work, and rubric-graded LLM-judge rewards. It gives double-digit gains on in-domain agent tasks at modest cost. Two lessons carry over. First, the regression on extraction shows that RL for multi-step task completion can hurt narrow precision skills, so keep those in the eval suite. Second, the reward is only as good as the rubrics; the expert-rubric investment is the real asset, not the training run. Compare R301, where Cognition applies the same idea to coding on Kimi K3, and R197 on Inkling 2 as a fine-tuning base.
