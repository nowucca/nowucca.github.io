Product management as one agentic funnel

Alex Barády's post, ENDGAME, 2026-11-18Published 2026-11-18Summarized 2026-11-18

Read the original

TL;DR

Once coding, review and release run on agents, the constraint moves upstream to deciding what to build. Alex Barády (founder of ENDGAME, an AI-transformation consultancy) proposes running product management as one agentic funnel: agents gather signals and score every candidate feature on cost, time to market and expected metric movement; a product lead picks one; two human gates (a decision record before build, an outcome review after release) bracket an agentic production line; and release metrics loop back as the next round's input.

Useful: the framing of the bottleneck shift, the two-gate shape, and the requirement that every option arrives pre-scored with the same three dimensions. Trust level: a consultancy's marketing post with one infographic. No case study, no numbers on cycle time or decision quality, no description of how the agents produce their estimates. Treat it as a design sketch, not evidence.

Source: LinkedIn post (2026-11-18, about 200 reactions).

The funnel

Read it left to right, then back along the bottom: agents turn raw signals into a backlog and score each candidate; a product lead picks one feature; Gate A turns it into a Product Slice with a decision record; an agentic line builds and releases it; Gate B reviews the outcome; and the measured metrics feed the next round as a new source. The post's infographic also maps today's agent coverage across nine stages: delivery (design through rollout) is typical today, epics and stories are partial, and strategy, roadmap and business case are the gap it sells.

What's new vs existing practice

Most of the funnel is standard product discovery (opportunity backlog, RICE-style scoring, a definition-of-ready gate, post-launch review). Three things are new or sharper:

  • Agents fill the scoring columns, not just the backlog. Every candidate arrives as "Feature · € · wk · Δ": cost, weeks to market, and expected movement in conversion, LTV and churn, weighted by strategic fit. Humans choose between pre-costed options instead of commissioning analysis per idea.

  • The stage map is a maturity claim. The infographic labels delivery (design, build, review, test, rollout) as typical today; epics and stories as partly agentic, not yet end to end; strategy, roadmap and business case as the new frontier; operate and maintain as next. That matches the industry pattern of agents moving left from code (see R058).

  • Ownership is split three ways. Agentic Product Management owns the funnel and both gates; Agentic Development turns the picked feature into a ready Product Slice (epic, stories, acceptance criteria); the Agentic Engineer runs the production line and returns evidence. The "returns evidence" line is the important one: the build stage reports back in the terms Gate B needs.

The "three clocks" diagnosis (research, prioritization and delivery run on different cadences) is the clearest idea in the post. Faster delivery exposes the mismatch; the proposed fix is to make all three one continuous loop driven by the same metrics.

Confident fake precision"Expected move in conversion, LTV and churn" is the hardest number in product work. An agent will always produce one, so every option looks equally well-founded.Require a range and the evidence behind each estimate; track estimate vs actual at Gate B and show calibration per estimator.
Optimizing what is measurableScoring on conversion, LTV and churn favors short-loop growth features over platform work, tech debt and bets whose payoff is slow.Keep a separate budget for strategic bets and tech debt, or weight by strategic fit that a human sets, not the agent.

Review checklist for an agentic PM funnel

  • Every candidate links back to the raw signals (tickets, interviews, analytics queries) that produced it.

  • Cost, time to market and value estimates are ranges with stated assumptions, not point values.

  • The Gate A decision record names the pick, the rejected alternatives, the success metric, the threshold and a kill condition.

  • A human sets the strategic-fit weights; the agent applies them.

  • Gate B compares the outcome to the Gate A prediction and logs the estimation error per dimension.

  • Estimate calibration is visible to the product lead before the next round.

  • Tech debt and platform work have a reserved share that the value score cannot crowd out.

  • The build stage returns evidence in the form Gate B needs (metric instrumentation shipped with the feature).

  • There is a cap on options per round and a written reason for each pick.
