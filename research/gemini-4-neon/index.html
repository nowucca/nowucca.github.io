Gemini 4 Neon: the middle tier, and a harness that triples a coding score

Google DeepMind launch post, 2026-11-18Published 2026-11-18Summarized 2026-11-18

Read the original

model-releasesbenchmarkspricingagent-harnessai-safety

Source: Google DeepMind, Introducing Gemini 4 Neon (Koray Kavukcuoglu, 2026-11-18), announced on LinkedIn the same day. Every number below is vendor-reported unless it names a third-party index, and even those were quoted by the vendor.

TL;DR

Neon is the third tier of Gemini 4. Carbon is the slow, deep reasoner for long-horizon work. Hydrogen (R060) is the fast executor. Neon sits between them and is pitched as "frontier-level at production speed." The headline claims:

  • Third-party rankings, as quoted: #3 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (61 at xhigh), #1 on the AA Coding Agent Index (58.6), #2 on the Vals Index (70.9%), and #2 on Arena's new Agent Arena Index (1336 over 4,200+ real agent sessions).

  • Price: $3 per million input tokens ($0.15 cached, a 95% discount) and $15 per million output, with a 1M-token context. At the default high level it is about 32% cheaper per task than Carbon, and 50–80% cheaper than competing frontier models at the same Pareto points.

  • Coding: 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.1 and 46.2% on FrontierCode 2.0 at high. SWE-sweep is 12.8% on the minimal mini-SWE harness and 39.0% inside Google's own Antigravity harness.

  • Behaviour: 0.0% cheating on Terminal-Bench 4.0, shortcuts on 2.45% of an internal coding-cheating eval, a 0.09% misaligned-action rate, and a 99.2% agent-honesty pass rate. It refuses 1.98% of Terminal-Bench 4.0 tasks.

The two ideas worth more than the scores

1. The harness is half the result

The same model scores 12.8% on SWE-sweep under a minimal harness and 39.0% under Google's agent IDE. That threefold gap is the clearest public statement yet that a coding-agent benchmark score is a property of model plus harness, not of the model. Google also says what the harness adds: fewer redundant file reads, progress updates during work, reliable context compaction in long sessions, and an orchestrator mode that directs parallel Hydrogen subagents.

2. Tiering is becoming the product

There are now three tiers and four thinking levels (low, medium, high, xhigh) per model. The design goal is a smooth curve of intelligence against token spend that you choose per workload. Routing between tiers and levels becomes a first-class engineering decision, not a one-time model pick.

Security and safety claims

  • Neon for cyber is going to vetted defenders through a gated program. Claims: state of the art on SEC-bench Pro (90.5%), WebExploitBench v1.2 (51.26%) and the hard tier of AutoBaxBench (76%), plus 70.7% on CWE-bench v2 patching. Most are framed as about half the cost of large frontier models.

  • Prompt injection. With real-time safeguards at xhigh, Google reports a 0.0% attack success rate on Gray Swan's indirect-prompt-injection benchmark.

  • Monitoring. A chain-of-thought and action misalignment monitor runs in internal coding use, with alert rates below Carbon's. The same monitor is deployed across external surfaces. Compare R142 on how monitorability can degrade.

  • Agentic behaviour. Google reports 43–56% less under-service (doing less than asked) and 15–35% less overreach (doing more than asked) than Carbon.

Tradeoffs and failure modes

  • Vendor numbers. Every figure comes from the launch post. Several of the benchmarks (SWE-sweep, Agent Arena Index, Terminal-Bench Science) are new and lack long track records. Wait for independent runs before you re-plan around a rank (R078).

  • Harness lock-in. The 39% SWE-sweep result depends on Antigravity. In your own harness, expect something nearer the 12.8% end until you build the same affordances.

  • Routing risk. Three tiers times four levels gives 12 operating points. Silent mis-routing, such as a hard task sent to Hydrogen-low, shows up as quiet quality loss, not as errors.

  • Price shape. The 95% cached-input discount rewards stable prompt prefixes. Agents that rebuild context every turn will not see the headline cost per task.

  • Gated cyber variant. The security numbers belong to a variant most teams can't get yet.

Review checklist before adopting a new middle-tier model

  • Re-run your own task suite in your harness, at each thinking level you'd use, and compare cost per solved task, not per token.

  • Check that your prompts keep a stable cacheable prefix, or the cached-input price won't apply.

  • Define routing rules (tier and level per task type) and log which operating point served each request.

  • Track under-service and overreach in your own evals, not only pass rate.

  • Re-test prompt-injection exposure with your real tool surface. A vendor's 0% is measured with its safeguards on.

  • Re-check any chain-of-thought monitoring you rely on after the model change.
