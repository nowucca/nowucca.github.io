A claimed VP ≠ VNP proof from a Claude model, formalized in Lean

Source: VP ≠ VNP Over Every Field, credited to Anthropic and Claude, alphaXiv AI Track, submitted 2026-11-23: alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.vp-vnp-arithmetic-lower-bounds. Surfaced by Stephan Janssen's post. Press coverage: CASRAI, FourWeekMBA, MACCUS.

Status: a claim. It has a Lean formalization but, per coverage, no completed human expert review yet.

What is claimed

  • Every arithmetic circuit that computes the permanent of an m×m matrix over the reals needs more than m^(c·(log m)^(1/3)) gates, equivalently 2^(c·(log m)^(4/3)), for some constant c > 0 and all large m.

  • The same bound holds over every field of characteristic other than two. Using the Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial in place of the permanent, it holds over every field.

  • Consequence: VP ≠ VNP over every field, settling Valiant's 1979 question in the algebraic model.

  • The previous best general lower bound for the permanent was about m² log m (the Strassen and Baur–Strassen style bound for a polynomial with m² variables and degree m). The new bound is superpolynomial but not exponential.

  • According to the abstract, an internal research Claude model found the result and it was formalized in Lean.

How the argument is structured

The paper does not attack the permanent directly. It studies auxiliary polynomials that encode networks of local constraints, proves those require large circuits, and then reduces: any small permanent circuit could be converted into a small circuit for the auxiliary polynomials. That is the standard shape of a lower-bound proof (hard core plus reduction). The novelty claimed is that it works for unrestricted circuits, with no limits on depth, constants or cancellation, which is where decades of restricted-model results stopped.

What it does not do

  • It does not settle P vs NP. Arithmetic circuits must compute the right polynomial formally, over the field and its extensions. Boolean circuits only need to be right on 0/1 inputs. There is no known transfer from this separation to P ≠ NP.

  • It is a quasi-polynomial-style bound, not an exponential one.

  • A Lean proof certifies the formal statement, not that the formal statement means what the English abstract says.

Engineering takeaways

  • Formal checking moves trust, it doesn't remove it. Trust shifts from the proof steps to the theorem statement, the definitions and the kernel. Review effort should go there.

  • Machine-found results need a review queue. As with code, generation outpaces human review. Pairing a formal checker with targeted expert review of statements and definitions is the scalable pattern.

  • Report honestly what was proved. The abstract's explicit note that this is not P vs NP is a good model for how AI-generated results should be scoped.

Failure modes to watch

  • Statement drift: the formal theorem is weaker or different from the headline.

  • Definitional shortcuts: a bespoke notion of circuit size or field that trivializes the bound.

  • Over-claiming downstream: coverage that reads VP ≠ VNP as progress on P vs NP.

Review checklist

  • Read the Lean statement before the abstract.

  • Print the axioms used and check for gaps.

  • Rebuild the proof in the pinned environment.

  • Compare definitions against standard references.

  • Wait for expert commentary before citing as settled.
