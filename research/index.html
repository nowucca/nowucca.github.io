A failure in one layer looks like another, so name the layer before blaming the model.

1.5Context economics and caching

1.6Multi-agent coordination

  1. 2026-11-10R019s3: agent swarms that learn their own coordinationN copies of one small model, no roles or orchestrator, just message/read tools in an async harness, trained with GRPO on one max-over-team reward. Learns broadcast-once for pooling private facts (93% vs 35% for online SFT at N=4) and branch-claiming for search. Per-agent reward breeds a public-goods dilemma where agents go silent. Caveats: toy tasks, 0.6B/1.7B models, no win over best-of-4 at training size after correction.

4.3Retrieval over long documents

  1. 2026-11-06R010PageIndex: reasoning-based retrieval for long documentsReplaces chunk-and-embed RAG with a per-document section tree. An agent reads the tree, then fetches tight page ranges and cites pages. In the repo's own 10-K notebook it scored 100% at about $110 per 1,000 queries; the best vector run was 92% at $134. Wins on long, structured documents; costs several strong-model turns per query and depends on a correct outline.

5. Harness tips

Practical advice on configuring and driving the agent harness itself: instructions, skills, memory, permission modes and prompting habits.

5.1Harness tips

  1. 2026-11-04R004The best Claude setup is no setupConfig written for older models rots, so wipe instructions, skills and memory, default to Auto, prompt with Goal + Context, and ask for HTML pages instead of .docx. Sound principle (prune and re-baseline after model upgrades), weak procedure: no before/after eval, and it deletes project facts and rationale along with obsolete capability patches.
    harness-configpromptingcontext-engineeringagent-memory
  2. 2026-11-09R018Ruben Hassid's six levels of Claude, checkedA consumer setup guide in six levels. Two claims hold against Anthropic's own pages: thumbs feedback stores the whole conversation for up to 5 years and may train models, and an owner turning org memory off permanently deletes every member's memory. Switching models mid-chat costs a cold cache, not a "re-read". Takeaways: pin model and effort per conversation, write a feedback-button policy, put the org memory toggle under change control, version config instead of wiping it.
    harness-configpromptingprompt-cachingagent-memoryagent-governancedata-privacy
  • AlsoR006 6 must-know terms for AI agent harnesses: what the harness does, as opposed to how you configure it; permissions decide when Auto is safe
  • AlsoR005 Second Brain OS: the five-layer map of AI agents: four rings built outside-in; approval prompts only for irreversible actions; harness bets expire, re-audit quarterly

6.2Workflows over agents

  1. 2026-11-08R015Psychology of programming and deterministic workflowsFraming matters more than programming: "agent" is a naming convention, and teams write hundreds of lines to steer one. Design the workflow instead, route with CLAUDE.md / AGENTS.md to Markdown step files, and use the model as the runtime. Sound reframe, but a model reading Markdown is not deterministic: that has to come from a fixed route, scripts, failing checks and placed human gates.

7. AI's effect on people

What happens to the people who use AI: what they learn, decide and get out of it, and where public-good deployments stand.

7.1Learning, advice and public-good deployment

  1. 2026-11-06R012AI crash projects for public good: Mollick's post and the evidence behind itMollick wants national crash projects in tutoring, health second opinions, financial literacy and government services using current models. The evidence says the bottleneck is the harness (steering toward augmentation, eliciting context, cheap oversight), not the model: students who let AI write kept nothing, and the medical agent was safe only with a physician watching live.
    ai-impacthuman-learningpublic-goodhuman-oversightevalsprompting
  • AlsoR014 MIT Technology Review's Intro to AI mini-course: a general-audience AI literacy course; its verify, iterate and know-when-AI-is-the-wrong-tool habits are what R012's harness lesson asks users to bring

7.2Human verification of AI output

  1. 2026-11-08R016Do humans still need to check AI's facts? (Mollick on BrowseComp and DeepFact)Mollick says human fact-checking of AI is an outdated default. The numbers hold, but BrowseComp tests finding, not checking, and DeepFact's error-heavy test set lets a constant answer score 83.9%. DeepFact's best result is humans auditing agent evidence (90.9%): redesign the human as an auditor of routed disagreements, measure reviewers with planted canaries, and version eval labels.
    fact-checkingbenchmarkshuman-oversightevalsai-impactagent-reliability
  • AlsoR017 Elephant-Goldfish Model: deep-context drafting, zero-context review: AI reviewers check the spec, the human triages routed findings and approves; about 30% of findings useful
  • AlsoR019 s3: agent swarms that learn their own coordination: swarm agents still act on unverified teammate reports; checking peers' claims is the paper's stated next step
