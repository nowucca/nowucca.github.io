1.2Self-improvement and learning loops

  1. 2026-11-11R024InfraRSI: contract-locked agents for training-throughput tuningA self-improving agent harness that speeds up distributed LLM training by searching launch settings and source patches together. The contribution is the verifier: before any edit, `init` locks a task contract (tokens per update, loss tolerance, allowed precisions) and hashes frozen files (data, loss, timers); `doctor` calibrates per-cluster timing noise; five invariant layers (execution, workload, schedule, numerical, timing) and back-to-back re-runs gate promotion, else the delivered speedup is 1.00×. Search keeps global time and censored peak-memory models over all trials (OOMs included) and a cross-hardware experience bank. Four dense and MoE workloads on 8 to 16 H100/H200: 1.83× geomean, mean MFU 17.4% to 30.0%, up to 2.75×; beats RE-Bench's scaffold (1.37×). Offline re-verification rejected 33% of 1,212 runs, and the LLM's own speedup predictions correlated negatively with measurements. Caveats: throughput only, some hacks caught only by human source review, four workloads, no replication.

  2. 2026-11-12R063No Second Run: recursive self-improvement applied to a frontier pre-training runClaims the first detailed account of RSI over a whole from-scratch pre-training run: the unrepeatable run is decomposed into smaller loops that can close (proxy runs, ablations, checkpoint evals).   3. 2026-11-13R066TRUST: a statistical acceptance gate for self-improving agent harnessesGreedy gates (keep a harness update if validation score rises) are fooled by sample noise and validation reuse: 45.59% of accepted updates were worse. TRUST defines a false evolution rate (share of accepted updates causing unacceptable loss) and controls it under a budget across the whole run, FDR-style, using a noninferiority test, overlap correction for reused eval data, e-values, and per-round error-budget allocation guided by an LLM reading the loop history. Proves the FER bound and a lower bound on final gain. HotpotQA, DROP, APPS with DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Qwen-3.8-Flash: bad acceptances 2.78%, higher final scores than conservative statistical gates. Caveats: more eval samples per decision, margin is a policy choice, short-horizon benchmarks, LLM inside the gate.

"Did this action help?" separates useful from useless actions; "will the task succeed?" does not. As the GRPO teacher, JEV lifts a Qwen2.5-1.5B policy from 72.8% to 82.9% (ALFWorld) and 56.8% to 71.4% (WebShop). Hindsight judging beats acting; a weak actor can be a good critic.
