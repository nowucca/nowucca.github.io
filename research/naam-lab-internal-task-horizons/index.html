Ramez Naam: lab-internal task horizons sit far below the METR-style charts

Authors Ramez Naam

Source: Ramez Naam, "The Most Important Charts in AI are Wrong" (2026-11-21). Found via TLDR AI 2026-11-23.

The claim

The charts people use to argue that AI self-improvement is close all extrapolate from benchmarks: METR's 80%-success task-length chart (up to about 4 hours for Claude Mythos Preview in early 2026), Benjamin Todd's mapping from the Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI) to METR horizons (30 to 40 hours), and the AI 2027 scenario (tasks of months by now). Naam argues that benchmarks select for clean, checkable tasks, and that the right test is how long a task AI can actually do inside the labs, on AI research itself.

His data and method

  • Month-by-month internal task-horizon data that OpenAI published (June to October; view inside OpenAI, jagged frontier), and one period from Google DeepMind's Gemini 4 Argon model card.

  • He compares each lab's internal horizon to the ECI-to-METR estimate for the same model.

  • He adjusts OpenAI's success rate for ambiguous or unsolvable tasks and tool errors, which makes his OpenAI figure higher than OpenAI's own.

  • For very high reliability he fits both a logistic curve and a capped log-linear curve, and uses their geometric mean.

Numbers

  • At 80% success, internal horizons are about 16 minutes (OpenAI, adjusted) and about 9 minutes (Google).

  • The model-matched ECI-to-METR estimates are 33x OpenAI's internal horizon and 41x Google's. AI 2027's projections are 111x and 114x.

  • The gap is widening: ECI-to-METR over OpenAI internal went from 53x in June to 165x in October.

  • Internal 50% horizon doubles about every 3.9 months. At 80% the exponential fit (5.6-month doubling) beats a straight line only 1.36 to 1.

  • At 99% success the horizon is about 28 seconds (OpenAI) and 6 seconds (Google).

  • His estimate of extra research productivity per ECI point is 3.6%, against a 15 to 19% threshold he previously estimated for runaway self-improvement.

His conclusion

Nothing in the internal data suggests runaway recursive self-improvement is near. Full automation of AI research needs tasks of days to weeks at very high reliability, which he puts many years out. He asks labs, Anthropic in particular, for OpenAI-level transparency, noting that METR's Anthropic assessment redacted its strongest evidence. He also says other indices give the same pattern, and that up to about 10% of internal tasks may be impossible.

How to read it

  • This is the strongest counterweight so far to METR's own Q4 update (R194). Both can be true: benchmark horizons are long and growing, and messy in-house horizons at 80%+ reliability are short. The gap between them is the real measurement.

  • The reliability curve matters most for engineering. A task horizon of hours at 50% but seconds at 99% is exactly the profile that demands verification loops and human checkpoints in agent pipelines.

  • Weaknesses: one lab's data for most of the series, a single Google point, his own adjustment to OpenAI's numbers, and curve fits extrapolated far beyond the data. The 2035 projections are speculative by his own label.
