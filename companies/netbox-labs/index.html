Snapshot

NetBox Labs is the commercial steward of NetBox, the open-source (Apache 2.0) network and infrastructure "source of truth" (~21.8k GitHub stars, nearly 400 contributors, "10,000+ organizations"). It sells NetBox Cloud (managed SaaS on AWS EKS), NetBox Enterprise (self-managed) and add-on products (Discovery/Orb, Diode, Assurance, Branching, Copilot, Asset Lifecycle, Validation, NDX, an MCP server), priced on the tools used and the size of the customer's infrastructure. In June 2026 it repositioned as an "infrastructure intelligence platform built for both humans and agents", and it now leans hard on AI data-center buildouts (NVIDIA, CoreWeave, neoclouds). It's a Series B startup, fully remote, about 125 to 150 people, with revenue in the single-digit millions (Aug 2025) and growing about 4x a year by the CEO's account.

History

  • 2016: Jeremy Stretch creates NetBox at DigitalOcean. It's later moved to the netbox-community GitHub org.
  • About 2021–22: NS1 (the DNS company co-founded by Kris Beevers) hires Stretch as a distinguished engineer and builds "NS1 Labs" around NetBox Cloud. Beevers: "we saw NetBox being used everywhere... We hired the creator of NetBox, Jeremy Stretch, and eventually spun out a new company." (Pulse 2.0, Feb 2026)
  • Jan–Feb 2023: NetBox Labs is spun out of NS1 just before IBM's acquisition of NS1 (announced late Feb 2023). CB Insights dates the spin-out to 2023-02-01. IBM stays on as an investor.
  • Apr 2023: $20M Series A led by Flybridge.
  • Jul 2025: $35M Series B led by NGP Capital.
  • Feb 2026: NetBox Copilot (AI assistant) becomes generally available.
  • Jun 2026: On NetBox's 10th anniversary, the company rebrands as an "Infrastructure Intelligence Platform" and adds NDX, Asset Lifecycle, Validation and an MCP server with agent skills.
  • Oct 2026: First NetBox Evolve conference (Kennedy Space Center, 2026-10-13), plus NVIDIA and Grafana partnerships. NetBox becomes the source of truth for NVIDIA Configuration Manager in DSX OS.

Ownership and status

Private, venture-backed. NetBox Labs, Inc. is a Delaware corporation headquartered in New York City, with no parent company. IBM holds a minority stake (investor since the spin-out). The open-source NetBox project is Apache 2.0, and NetBox Labs employs its creator and lead maintainer.

Financials

  • Revenue: "in the single-digit millions" (Beevers to B17/Business Insider, Aug 2025).
  • Growth: "more than 4x over the past 12 months on top of 4x the previous year" (Beevers, Pulse 2.0, Feb 2026). If that holds, revenue is probably now in the low tens of millions (my estimate).
  • Customers: "roughly 200" paying customers (Aug 2025), including ARM, Cisco, CoreWeave, J.P. Morgan, Kaiser Permanente, Riot Games, Constant Contact and state DOTs. "Dozens of Fortune 500." "A third of customers use multiple NetBox Labs products" (June 2026). The "10,000+ organizations" figure counts open-source users, not customers.
  • Profitability: not disclosed. They're presumably burning cash.

Engineering

  • Org shape: It's going from a single product to a multi-product platform, delivered both as SaaS and on-prem. Teams visible in job posts: NetBox Core/Core Apps, Cloud Delivery (the SaaS platform on AWS EKS running "thousands of customer instances"), NetBox Delivery (on-prem/Enterprise), Foundations (auth, SSO, licensing, billing), Observability (Orb, telemetry), Discovery, Assurance and Integrations. Architecture runs through SIGs and RFCs. A Principal Engineer reports to the CTO.
  • Tech stack: Python/Django (NetBox core), Go (Diode, Orb agent, observability backends), C++ (pktvisor), gRPC/protobuf, PostgreSQL, Redis/Redis Streams, ClickHouse, Kafka/MQTT/AMQP, OpenTelemetry, Prometheus/Mimir/Loki/Grafana, Kubernetes (EKS), AWS, Terraform, Helm and GitHub Actions. Job ads explicitly expect AI-assisted development (Copilot, Cursor, Claude) and the VP Eng post mentions "spec-driven development, AI-assisted implementation and review."
  • Open source (GitHub, read 2026-11-05): netbox-community/netbox has 21.8k stars and the org has 28 repos, including netbox-docker (2.8k), devicetype-library (1.6k) and pynetbox (690). The netboxlabs org has 29 repos: pktvisor (526, C++), netbox-mcp-server (254, Python), diode (167, Go), netbox-branching (159), orb-agent (107, Go) and netbox-custom-objects (99). Signals for a candidate

Green flags

  • It owns the de facto open-source standard in its niche. That's a real moat, and its OSS is healthy (21.8k stars, very active repos).
  • The founder has done this before: Beevers took NS1 to an IBM exit, and much of the NS1 leadership came with him.
  • Revenue was reported to grow about 4x in each of the last two years. Headcount is doubling yearly. Hiring more than doubled in 2026.
  • It's well placed for the AI data-center buildout (NVIDIA, CoreWeave, neoclouds), and it has a credible agent/MCP story grounded in its data.
  • Fully remote, with posted salary ranges and equity for everyone. Engineering is about half the company.

Red flags / risks

  • Revenue is still small (single-digit millions as of Aug 2025), and $55M has been raised in total. Runway depends on hitting the growth story, and the next raise is probably due in 2026–27.
  • The company is going from one product to many on both SaaS and on-prem. Job ads and reviews describe ambiguity, rework and systems "not fully formed".
  • There's built-in tension between open source and commercial: Core is Apache 2.0 and the "Community first" value constrains what can be monetized.
  • Positioning leans heavily on AI data centers, which exposes it to that capex cycle.
  • The Glassdoor sample is tiny and sales-heavy, so there's no real engineering-culture signal yet.

Questions to ask in interviews

  • Current ARR and growth, gross margin, runway in months, and when they plan to raise next.
  • Who is VP Engineering now, how many engineers are there today, and how are teams split across SaaS, on-prem, OSS core and observability?
  • How do they decide what goes into open-source NetBox versus the commercial products?
  • What share of revenue comes from AI and neocloud customers versus traditional enterprise?
  • What does "AI-native engineering" mean day to day: tools, review practices, and how it's measured?
  • Remote across the US and UK: core hours, how often offsites happen, and on-call expectations.
