Company profile

Resolve AI

"AI for prod": an AI SRE that goes on call, triages alerts and investigates incidents, built by the ex-Splunk Observability founders.

Researched 2026-11-06WebsiteLinkedInCareers

Name check: this is Resolve AI at resolve.ai (San Francisco, CEO Spiros Xanthos). It is not Resolve Systems (resolve.io, IT automation), Rezolve AI (rezolve.ai, Nasdaq: RZLV, commerce AI) or ResoluteAI. Glassdoor and news searches mix these up.

Not covered this pass: LinkedIn company pages and SEC EDGAR (the scripts weren't run; a direct EDGAR query from the cloud returned nothing), Glassdoor and Levels.fyi (both refused automated reads).

Snapshot

Resolve AI sells an agentic "AI SRE" platform to large engineering organizations: it takes on-call shifts, triages alerts, investigates and helps fix production incidents, and runs routine operational tasks, working in Slack, Teams and the terminal. Customers named publicly include Coinbase, DoorDash, MongoDB, MSCI, Salesforce and Zscaler (Series A post), with Cisco, SAP, Snowflake, Adobe, Robinhood, Expedia and Toast also on the homepage. It is sold as an enterprise contract with no published price (We The Flywheel, 2026-05). Stage: Series A (extended), $1.5B valuation, more than $190M raised, about two years old, "125+" people as of February 2026.

Mission, purpose, vision, values

  • Mission: "We're an applied AI lab on a mission to help every engineer run production effortlessly." (careers page)
  • Goal: "Our goal is to help every engineer operate production fluently without being bottlenecked by context, expertise, or tools." (Introducing AI for prod, 2026-02-04)
  • Tagline: "Machines on-call for humans." (homepage)
  • Values (careers page):

    • High Trust, Low Ego: "We welcome constructive dissent, assume good intentions, and leave our titles at the door."

History

  • ~2006: Spiros Xanthos and Mayank Agarwal meet in grad school at UIUC (About).
  • 2019: Their previous company, Omnition, is acquired by Splunk; they go on to lead Splunk Observability (Agarwal as its chief architect). The company also credits them with a prior exit to VMware and with co-creating OpenTelemetry (TechCrunch, About; the VMware and OpenTelemetry claims are the company's own).
  • Early 2024: co-founded, per TechCrunch. The company's own About page says "Founded October, 2024", which matches its emergence from stealth.
  • 2024-10: out of stealth with a $35M seed led by Greylock.
  • 2026-02-04: $125M Series A at $1B; repositions as "AI for prod".
  • 2026-04-16: $40M Series A extension at $1.5B; launches Resolve AI Labs.
  • 2026-08-18: hires Jason Forget (ex-Cockroach Labs) as President and founding CRO.

Ownership and status

Private, venture-backed, headquartered in San Francisco with a New York presence for sales and forward-deployed roles. Legal entity and state of incorporation weren't confirmed (EDGAR not checked this pass; a Form D search for "Resolve AI" would pin both down). Major holders by round: Greylock, Lightspeed, DST Global, Salesforce Ventures, Unusual Ventures, plus angels including Jeff Dean, Fei-Fei Li, Reid Hoffman and Amjad Masad (About).

  • ARR about $4M in December 2025, per two anonymous sources (TechCrunch). Not confirmed by the company. That is roughly a 250× revenue multiple at the $1B price.

  • Customer outcome claims: Coinbase 72% less time to investigate critical incidents; Zscaler 30% fewer engineers per incident (company).

Engineering

  • Product surface: On-call, Incidents, Operational tasks, AI SRE and Custom agents; integrates via MCP, APIs and "Skills"; extends production context to coding agents (blog, 2026-09-01) and runs background agents for proactive prod tasks (2026-07-17).
  • Resolve AI Labs (2026-04): domain-specific models and post-training, reasoning over telemetry, evals, synthetic data and simulated environments, guardrails.
  • Stack: little is published. The Staff+ Backend posting calls for AWS and Kubernetes plus "databases and messaging systems" and describes building simulation environments to train SRE agents; no languages named (Built In posting). Visa sponsorship offered.
  • Blog themes (55 posts, mostly product marketing): agent harness design, accuracy gains ("The Failures That Led to 2x Agent Accuracy Improvement"), token efficiency, human-in-the-loop fatigue, why DIY agents fail at incident investigation (blog).
  • Open engineering roles (SF unless noted): Software Engineer Backend, Frontend, Infrastructure Security; Research Engineer; Growth Engineer; Forward Deployed Engineer (SF and NY) (careers, read 2026-11-06).
  • Open source: no notable public repos found; the founders' open-source pedigree is OpenTelemetry.

Signals for a candidate

Green flags

  • Proven founders who have built and sold in exactly this domain (Omnition to Splunk, ran Splunk Observability).
  • Well funded: $165M raised in 2026, top-tier investors, no runway pressure.
  • Real enterprise logos (Coinbase, DoorDash, Salesforce, Zscaler) and a serious research bet (Labs, ex-Meta Llama lead).
  • Hard, interesting engineering: agent harnesses, evals, simulation environments, telemetry reasoning at enterprise scale.

Red flags and risks

  • Valuation far ahead of revenue (~$4M ARR reported at $1B); the next round has to show big ARR growth.
  • Crowded category: Datadog Bits AI SRE, PagerDuty, incident.io, Rootly, plus well-funded startups Traversal and Cleric. Incumbents own the telemetry.
  • New President/CRO from outside signals a push to scale sales fast; expect pressure and possible reorgs.
  • Office-first in SF with little remote flexibility; NY is mostly field roles.
  • No independent employee reviews or pay data to check culture claims.

Questions to ask

  1. What is ARR now, and what does the plan to the next round look like?
  2. How is engineering organized between product, Labs and forward-deployed work? What's the engineering headcount?
  3. How do you win against Datadog and PagerDuty, who already have the customer's telemetry and alerts?
  4. How do you measure agent accuracy in production, and who owns evals?
  5. What is the in-office expectation for engineers, and is there any remote or NY-based engineering?
  6. What is the stack (languages, data stores), and how much do engineers rotate through customer deployments?
  7. Equity: strike price at a $1.5B preferred price, and what's the refresh policy?
