Close with the one-year success question

At "any questions for us?", ask them to picture you a year in, already a great hire, and tell you what you did.

Original Ask This Question At The End Of An Interview To Get Hired! (YouTube Short) by Charlie Houpert, via Motivational Hub

closingquestions to askbehavioral

The idea

When the interviewer asks if you have questions, ask: "Let's imagine this interview went really well and it's a year from now. What would I have done to excel in the role for you to think it was a good decision?"

It does two jobs at once. The interviewer has to picture you in the role and already succeeding, which flips the frame from "should we hire this person?" to "here's what this person did for us". And their answer is the real success criteria for the job, often more honest than the posting, which you can reply to on the spot and reuse in follow-ups.

How to use the answer

  • Listen for the one or two outcomes they name first. Those are what the job is really about.
  • Map one story to it right away: "That's close to what I did at my last company when…", in two or three sentences, not a second interview.
  • Note any gap they hint at (a team that's struggling, a system that keeps paging) and say how you'd approach it in the first 90 days.
  • Reuse their words in the thank-you note and in later rounds with other interviewers.

Variants by interviewer

InterviewerTweak
Hiring managerAsk it as written. They own the decision.
Peer engineer"…for you to be glad I joined the team?"
Founder or exec"…for it to have clearly moved the business?"
Recruiter"What has the hiring manager said great looks like a year in?"

Ask it last, once, and then stop talking. Don't stack it with three other questions.

Say it like this

  • Let's imagine this interview went really well and it's a year from now. What would I have done to excel in the role for you to think it was a good decision?
  • That makes sense. Getting a new customer from first connector to a trusted on-call answer is very close to what I did when… and in the first 90 days I'd want to start with…

Bring it up when they ask

  • Do you have any questions for us?
  • What would success look like for you in this role?
  • Is there anything else you'd like us to know?

Where it fits

Resolve AI
For the Forward Deployed Engineer role, the answer will likely be named customer deployments and evals. Tie your story to how you'd measure an agent's answers in production.
ParentSquare
For the Principal Engineer role, expect answers about the monolith and the engineering leadership gap. Reply with a technical-direction story.
BetterUp
Useful for finding out what the AI coaching push actually needs from engineering, since public signals are mixed.
LaunchDarkly
With no open role yet, a recruiter version works: "If a role opens, what would make someone a clear win a year in?"
